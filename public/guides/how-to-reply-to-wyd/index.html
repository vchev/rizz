12 replies to "wyd" that keep the conversation going

Updated 2026-11-05

Short answer: "wyd" is an invitation, not a question. Answer in one line with something real you're doing, then hand it back with a hook she can grab. Skip "nm u?", which ends the chat.

Why "wyd" is easy to fumble

"wyd" (what you doing) usually means I'm bored and thought of you. The worst reply is a flat report ("nothing, just chilling"). The best reply gives her something to react to.

12 replies, by mood

Easy and honest

  1. "Making pasta and pretending I know what al dente means. You?"
  2. "Just got back from the gym, so mostly lying on the floor. Wbu"
  3. "Rewatching The Office for the 4th time. Don't judge"

A little playful

  1. "Thinking about whether to text you first. You beat me to it"
  2. "Losing an argument with my roommate about pineapple on pizza. Which side are you on?"
  3. "Trying to pick a show and failing. Give me your best recommendation"

Moving toward a plan

  1. "Not much, but I'm free Thursday if you want to do nothing together"
  2. "About to grab tacos. Have you tried that place on 5th?"
  3. "Deciding if I'm a weekend hiker. Know any good trails?"

Late at night

  1. "Should be sleeping, am not. You too apparently"
  2. "Lying in bed reading the same page 3 times. What's keeping you up?"
  3. "Just finished a long day. Tell me something good"

The rule behind every good reply

Real detail + a question or choice she can answer in one tap. That's it. Pickup lines and generic compliments read as copy-pasted, and in one 2026 survey, 1 in 5 singles said they'd ghosted someone whose texts felt AI-written (Arrows survey). Sounding like yourself matters more than sounding clever.

FAQ

Is "wyd" flirty? Often, especially late at night. Match the energy: a playful "wyd" gets a playful reply. Should I ask "wyd" back? Only after you've shared something. "Nm, wyd?" puts all the work on her. What if she only replies "nm"? Give her one easy opening ("Same. Best thing you ate today?"). If it stays one-word, see what to text a one-word answerer.
