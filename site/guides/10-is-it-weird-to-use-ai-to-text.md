**Short answer:** Using AI for ideas is common now. What people dislike is texts that sound fake. Use AI like a friend you ask for a second opinion, and only send what sounds like you.

## How common it is
- 26% of US singles used AI to help with dating in 2025, and 37% of them used it to draft first messages ([Match and Kinsey Institute, Singles in America](https://www.androguider.com/2026/06/ai-and-love-why-nearly-half-of-singles.html)).
- In a February 2026 survey of 1,008 US singles, 54% had used AI for some part of dating ([Arrows](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/arrows-survey-ai-sees-widespread-use-and-dislike-in-dating/)).

## Why it can backfire
In the same survey, 58% called AI-written chats "digital catfishing", and 1 in 5 had ghosted someone they suspected of using AI. The giveaway is usually generic, overly polished lines that could be sent to anyone.

## How to use it without being weird
1. **Keep your voice.** Your length, your slang, your emoji (or none).
2. **Use what she actually said.** A reply that references her message can't be copy-paste.
3. **Edit before sending.** Treat suggestions as a starting point.
4. **Never pretend to be someone else.** If you meet, you're the same person who texted.

## FAQ
**Will she know?** Not if it sounds like you and fits the conversation.
**Is it dishonest?** Asking a friend what to say isn't. Pretending to be someone you're not is.
